# Thaelyzovrak

> A map of black feathers remembers the island erased from the morning sky.

## The Cartographer's Empty Wall

The cartographer keeps one wall bare. Visitors assume it is reserved for a great work, but she says the wall is already occupied. She cleans its empty surface each morning and leaves a bowl of clear water beneath it.

Her other maps are drawn on ordinary paper. They show roads, hills, rivers, and towns whose names can be found in travel books. The bare wall shows its country only after somebody has left the room.

She knows this because the bowl reflects what she cannot see directly. In its water lies an island covered in black trees. Above the trees hangs a pale shape resembling a second island turned upside down.

The water remains still until a visitor asks where the country is. Then a small wave reaches the bowl's edge, and the cartographer closes her eyes until it passes.

## A Sailor Who Had Lost His Shore

A sailor arrived carrying a feather inside a wooden case. He had found it on a beach after a night in which every star disappeared except one. In the morning, his boat stood on dry land, facing a sea that had moved beyond the horizon.

He wanted a map back to the water. The cartographer set his case beside the bowl. The feather rose without being touched and pressed itself against the bare wall.

Where it rested, a line of black trees appeared. Their branches carried little white boats. Beneath them ran a path leading to a house with an open door.

The sailor recognized the house. He had drawn it in the margins of letters throughout his life, though he had never known where he first saw it. Each drawing contained the same open doorway and the same empty place beside the gate.

## A Coast Drawn From the Other Side

The cartographer offered him a pencil made from compressed ash. He placed its point against the wall and drew the beach from memory. The line moved ahead of his hand, bending toward places he did not remember.

A harbor appeared. Beyond it, a tower leaned toward the water. In the tower's highest window stood a person holding a lantern covered with cloth.

The sailor stopped drawing. His hand remembered the lantern's weight. He remembered waiting for a boat, hiding the light when voices approached, and promising someone that the island would still be there by morning.

He had never written those memories in a letter. He had never allowed himself to finish the picture beyond the open door.

The feather on the wall divided into seven narrow shadows. Each shadow became a river crossing the island toward its center.

## The Island's Sleeping Shape

At the meeting of the rivers lay a dragon with its wings folded beneath the soil. Its spine formed the hills. Its horns rose as the two towers at the harbor entrance. The black trees grew between its scales.

The pale island above was its reflection, held in a sky that had once been water. When it dreamed, the reflection drifted. When it stirred, sailors forgot which side of the horizon their ships belonged to.

The cartographer had been watching the reflection for years. She kept her wall bare because ink would have fixed the island in the wrong place. Only the feather could draw a coastline that changed while remaining true.

The sailor looked into the bowl. Beside the open door stood a figure carrying the empty wooden case. A black feather lay at the figure's feet, pointing toward the harbor.

## An Inscription in the Ash

The pencil crumbled between his fingers. On the wall, its dust arranged itself into four groups:

*Thae — lyzo — vrak — unsha.*

The cartographer spoke them toward the bowl. Water rose into a thin vertical sheet. Through it, the harbor lantern became visible beneath its cloth, bright enough to cast a small shadow across the workshop floor.

The sailor lifted the covering. He did not remember crossing the room, yet his hand was holding the lantern now. Its light showed the island's reflection descending toward the black trees.

Below them, the sleeping dragon opened its wings just enough to reveal a strip of pale sand. A boat waited there, turned toward the sea. In its stern sat someone whose face the sailor no longer needed to imagine.

## The Map That Was Not Kept

He placed the lantern beside the water and stepped toward the thin sheet. The cartographer heard the scrape of a boat leaving shore. Then the bowl was ordinary water again, and the wall had become bare.

The wooden case remained on her table. Inside lay a drawing of the house, finished at last. The doorway was closed, a little smoke rose from the chimney, and two shadows rested beside the gate.

She did not frame the drawing. She placed it in a drawer and continued making maps of roads, hills, and towns whose names could be read aloud.

Each morning, she cleans the empty wall and fills the bowl.

Sometimes its reflection shows a distant sail. Behind it, an island keeps its place beneath a sky that has remembered how to hold the sea.
